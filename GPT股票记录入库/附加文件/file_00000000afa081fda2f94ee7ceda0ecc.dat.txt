1. Introduction - The Opportunity for an
Enterprise-Grade Layer 3 Product
Angora Networks has built its Ethernet networking business with SAGA Telecom as a
foundational manufacturing partner. Together, we cover a strong breadth of the market —
from unmanaged edge switches through capable L2+ and L3-managed aggregation devices.
As we scale our ambitions in the Turkish and regional enterprise and public-sector markets,
including our current engagement in the Turk Telekom 2026 procurement cycle, we see a
clear and exciting opportunity: extending SAGA's already strong portfolio upward into a true
enterprise-grade, high-end Layer 3 tier.
Today, SAGA's most capable models (the STC-424XGGPM / STC-448XGGPM family) are
well positioned in the L2+ / entry-L3 segment, with static routing as their core routing
capability. Every enterprise tender we have reviewed this year, and the direct customer
feedback we have gathered in the field, converges on four requirements for a true enterprise
L3 tier — requirements that represent a natural and achievable next step for SAGA's product
roadmap:

reasonably command a 2–4x list-price uplift over SAGA's current flagship product, while still
remaining attractively priced against established mid-market competitors — thanks to
SAGA's existing OEM manufacturing economics.

A further requirement for the enterprise tier is software parity across the full 8-port, 24-port,
and 48-port range: all three form factors should run the same Layer 3 feature set (OSPF,
BGP, VRF, VRRP, ECMP/BFD, etc.), not a reduced subset on smaller units. This also
extends to mixed-model stacking — for example, a 24-port unit and a 48-port unit should be
able to join the same stack — which is a common deployment pattern in campus
wiring-closet designs where port density needs vary floor-by-floor within a single logical
switch stack.
We recognise that stacking, in particular, represents a significant R&D commitment — it is
the capability that most clearly distinguishes a genuine enterprise/core-tier switch from an
advanced access-tier switch, and it is the single feature most consistently requested in the
tenders and customer conversations we are seeing. We would welcome the opportunity to
discuss joint engineering input, field requirements gathering, and — should it be useful —
co-investment or volume-commitment structures that make this development path
commercially attractive for SAGA.
3. On the Long-Term Partnership
Angora Networks views SAGA Telecom as our foundational manufacturing partner in the
Ethernet networking business line, and this recommendation is offered in that spirit — as an
invitation to grow together into a segment we believe SAGA is well placed to lead.
Our two companies have already built real trust: SAGA in EXW pricing discipline, product
reliability, and responsiveness to customisation requests; Angora in bringing that portfolio to
the Turkish enterprise, public-sector, and service-provider market with local sales, support,
and regulatory relationships. Closing the enterprise L3 gap converts this into a genuinely
differentiated joint offering — one where Angora can compete for larger, higher-margin,
multi-year framework contracts (such as the Turk Telekom 2026 tender and its successors),
with SAGA product positioned at the centre of the technical solution.
